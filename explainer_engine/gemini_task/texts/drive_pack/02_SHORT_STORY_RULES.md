# Rules for the short story

Sources: `docs/STORY_ENGINE.md` (ST-01..ST-10, ST-24, SC-11, SC-12, YT-01, YT-02); `docs/ORCHESTRATION.md` (OR-15);
the creator's notes of 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-20 in `docs/STORY_ENGINE.md`.

The short story is the episode in plain words, line by line, before any script exists: what happens, who wants what,
who says what to whom, how it ends. The creator reads it on a phone and says yes or no. So it must be short, concrete
and complete.

## What to check

Check in this order. Stop being strict about craft when the story itself does not hold: say that first.

**A. Is there a story worth telling?**
1. One idea a viewer would repeat to a friend. You can say it in one sentence.
2. One dramatic question, yes or no, asked at the start and answered at the end (ST-01).
3. A promise: what the viewer gets for staying. The ending pays it, and the ending has a kind: twist, reversal,
   bookend, callback or open question (ST-10).

**B. Does it move?**
4. The parts are joined by BUT (an obstacle) or THEREFORE (a consequence), never by "and then" (ST-02).
5. Every part after the opening says what is lost if it goes wrong, and something changes in every part (ST-03).
6. At least two turning points, and one of them in the middle third (ST-07).
7. Something planted early decides the end (ST-06). Open questions are closed, in order (ST-05).
8. The opening is a paradox, a high stake or a sharp question, never a date and the weather (YT-01). Parts end on
   something unresolved that pulls forward, never on a summary (YT-02).

**C. Are there people in it?**
9. Named heroes who want something, with a flaw; the lead also changes (ST-04). A main hero and secondary heroes, like
   a series, not ten equal faces.
10. The turns are played by the heroes: somebody acts or speaks. A turn told only by the narrator over a map is a
    defect (ST-24).
11. Real exchanges are marked: who says what to whom, and their wants collide.
12. A mini-film, not a montage of moments. Mixed outcomes are welcome: some adapt, some do not.

**D. Is it honest?**
13. Each part is labelled fact, reasoned or invented, and invention is never the majority (ST-09). A part labelled
    fact names its claims.
14. For every factual sentence you doubt: open the episode's claim map first. If the claim is there with a source,
    check the sentence against that source. If it is not there, say so and give the source you found.
15. The what-if follows from its cause: given the change the story makes, would this really follow? Say where the
    logic jumps.

**E. Does it keep the two strict rules?**
16. No trait statements, ratings or biographies from the narrator (SC-12).
17. Nothing steps outside the story (SC-11).

## What good advice looks like here

Name the weakest part and say what would make it stronger, in one or two sentences, with the reason. One strong
suggestion is worth more than ten small ones. Do not rewrite the story.
