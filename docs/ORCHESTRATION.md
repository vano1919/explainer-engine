# Orchestration: how an episode is run end to end, alone, with money and stops under control

Canonical for the autonomous run of a whole episode (creator, 2026-10-16: "fully autonomous, phases, find mistakes early, a
hard cap, a stop must never lose material"). The agent that follows it is `.claude/agents/orchestrator.md`; the code that
enforces the money and stop rules is `explainer_engine/core/ledger.py`. Gates of one video stay in
[PRODUCTION_RULES](PRODUCTION_RULES.md); the script format in [PRODUCTION_SCRIPT](PRODUCTION_SCRIPT.md).

## Rules (OR-xx)

| ID | Rule | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|
| OR-01 | **Phases with gates.** Cheap, free checks come first; the first paid call is a one-item test (one read chunk, one picture); the expensive batch starts only after the test passed its check. A failed gate stops the run at that phase. | orchestrator, `ledger.Run.phase` |
| OR-02 | **Hard caps in code.** Every paid service has a cap written in the run ledger before the run (`Run.cap`), a charge is recorded before the call (`Run.reserve`) and refused past 80 % of the cap; a refusal ends the phase safely, it never raises through a half-finished batch. | `core/ledger.py` |
| OR-03 | **A stop loses nothing.** Each item (a read chunk, a picture) is written to disk and recorded in `run.json` as soon as it is paid; a re-run skips recorded items, so an interruption, a cap stop or a crash resumes at the first missing item. Paid results are never re-bought. | `ledger.Run.done`, `Run.pending` |
| OR-04 | **Ordered fallbacks, not retries.** A failed paid call is not repeated blindly. Three failures on the same item or phase stop it (for code the counter is `jobs/<phase>/attempts.json`, `core/ledger.Attempts`: attempts 1-2 at the assigned level, the third only escalated to Opus or the reviewer's plan, then the item stops): write the cause from evidence, fix it in the repo with a test, record it in the feedback log, then continue with the other phases that do not depend on it. | `ledger.Run.fail` |
| OR-05 | **Nothing public.** The one upload path is the unlisted/private staging of D-57 (`publish stage`), run per piece as soon as it passes its own tests (PRODUCTION_RULES standing order 9), after the creator's recorded approval (PC-08) and the QA gates (PC-09); never public, no comment on the channel. The run ends with files, a report and unlisted links. | invariant 4, D-57 |
| OR-06 | **No pod without a spoken yes.** A pod needs the creator's approved price and time cap for that pod and starts only through `cloud/runpod.py create` (ceilings in `config/spend_caps.json`, guard, ledger; D-56); `runpod check` runs at session start and end (hooks). A cap or a surprise near 80 % stops acting and waits. | TMR money rules |
| OR-07 | **Look at the output.** Every phase ends by opening what it made (contact sheet, listening to chunk joins, a sample frame) and writing one line of evidence in the report, never only "tests pass". | report |
| OR-08 | **Morning report.** One short Georgian message: files and links, minutes, credits and dollars per service against the caps, balances read, what failed and what was fixed, what needs the creator. | orchestrator |
| OR-11 | **One episode rulebook binds every step.** After the topic and format are known the `episode-standards-director` writes `episode_rules.json` (provisional), finalises it before the script is committed, and the orchestrator passes `python -m explainer_engine.standards brief <rules> --role <role>` to research, script, voice, image, music, sound, edit, metadata and QA; no step invents its own legal policy, licence assumption or art direction; conflicts come back to the orchestrator, which decides or asks the creator. | `standards/rulebook.py`, [EPISODE_STANDARDS](EPISODE_STANDARDS.md) ES-18 |
| OR-12 | **Rechecks at the irreversible steps.** The rulebook gate runs at `script_lock`, before any paid or high-cost generation (`paid_assets`) and at `final_qa`; a changed script or rulebook re-checks only the affected rules and stamps again; a BLOCK or an undecided REVIEW stops that step, not the whole episode. | `standards gate`/`stamp` (ES-16), `production/paint.py`, `production_gate` |
| OR-13 | **Every executor has a contract** (table below): it receives the slice of the plan it executes (shot, line, sound event), the rulebook brief for its role, references and limits, and returns its output with provenance; it never invents another scene, voice direction or art direction. | this table, `standards brief` |
| OR-14 | **Code before prose.** A rule that can be checked by code is checked by code: schemas, validators (PV, ES, ENG, HY), the run ledger, and the PreToolUse hook on the ElevenLabs connector (`.claude/settings.json`). | tests |
| OR-15 | **A short scenario before the script** (creator 2026-10-19). Before any production script is written or rewritten, the creator gets a short scenario: the story line by line in plain words, the dialogue marked with who says it and the faces, and what changes against the last version. The script is written only after the creator approves it; the approval is recorded in `episode.scenario` (file, who, date, sha256), and a scenario edited later is approved again. | `scripting/scenario.py` (PV-45) |
| OR-16 | **One variant collage before a new hero or world** (creator 2026-10-19). Before a hero or the episode's world (colours, buildings, mood) is painted for the first time, ONE picture shows 4-6 variants side by side (a free sheet of pictures that already exist when there are any); the creator picks a tile and that picture becomes the reference for every later picture and face sheet. Exploration never costs a picture per variant. The pick is recorded in `cast.<id>.reference` and `look.environment` (collage, tile, picture, approved_by, approved_at). The creator may waive the collage for a minor hero (2026-10-20, "not worth drawing just to show"): `first_picture: true`; the hero's first scene picture is shown on the contact sheet and becomes the reference after his OK. | `scripting/reference_approval.py` (PV-47) |
| OR-17 | **The creator approves the finished script before anything is paid** (creator 2026-10-20). The order is scenario -> creator's yes (OR-15) -> the full production script written to every rule (writers' room, editorial gate) -> the creator reads the finished script -> creator's yes (approval #1) -> only then a paid read, picture or music. The yes is recorded with `python -m explainer_engine.scripting.script_approval approve <script> --by <creator>` in `episode.script_approval` (who, date, sha256 of every word said); a line changed afterwards sends the script back to the creator. | `scripting/script_approval.py` (PV-53), paint preflight, `standards/hook.py`, `production_gate` 0.9 |
| OR-18 | **Library first** (creator 2026-10-20, D-76). Before any new background music, sound effect or reusable picture is generated, the reusable libraries (sounds `assets/audio/library`, pictures `assets/pictures/library`) are searched by tags and the search is recorded in the episode's `asset_reuse.json` (`python -m explainer_engine.assets_reuse <episode> --kind music\|sfx\|vocal\|picture --tags ... --decide reuse\|generate_new`); choosing `generate_new` over matches needs a written reason. New is generated only when nothing fits, and what is generated is registered back into the library (`audio/register.py`, `imagegen/picture_library.py add`) so the next episode finds it. Narration is episode-bound and exempt. | `standards/hook.py` (a paid music/SFX call without the record is refused); `paint plan` lists a prompt's library matches (PP-08) |
| OR-19 | **A two-part sample before the rest** (creator 2026-10-21; it stages the 2026-10-20 combined images-and-audio approval, which stays one estimate and one provider choice). After the script approval and the combined estimate, paid generation covers the sample first: the first two segments' voice and pictures (made in parallel), rendered and stitched into one piece the creator watches. His notes are fixed, the sample re-rendered, and only his recorded word — `python -m explainer_engine.production.sample approve --by --ref`, written to `sample_approval.json` with the script's sha256 — opens paid voice and pictures for the rest of the video and the Shorts. `estimate_only`, reusable channel assets (face sheets, references, `reusable` plans) and library sounds stay allowed; new paid music and sound effects are kept to the sample by this rule and the library-first gate. A script edited after the approval needs a new sample approval. | `production/sample.py`, `paint.preflight` (PP-09), `standards/hook.py`, `production_gate` gate 6.5 |
| OR-20 | **One main agent and one critic** (creator 2026-10-25, D-79). A production run (an episode, a LoRA training) is driven by one main agent (`orchestrator`, `lora-coordinator`, or the session itself) that runs every stage itself, following the stage pages in `.claude/agents/`; the stage gates (`stage_guard`, validators, KG, the ledger) stay as they are. The only agent it spawns is the one independent critic of that run (`script-critic` for scripts, `lora-checkpoint-judge` for checkpoints), with a fresh context and without the maker's notes. No other stage is spawned as its own agent. | `tests/unit/test_agent_roles.py` (stage pages marked, only the two main agents may spawn, exactly the two critics) |
| OR-21 | **Reusable before fast** (creator 2026-10-25 23:33 Tbilisi: "reusable vs speed: reusable every time", 23:34: "if the cost is the same"). When a step can be done as a quick one-off (a script in the scratchpad, a hand-typed command, a copy) or as a reusable tool (a module or CLI in the repo with a test, a pod script and config kept in the repo), the reusable path is taken even when it is slower, as long as it costs no more money; a cheaper one-off is put to the creator. Pods start only through the repo's `cloud/runpod.py create`, with the pod script and the training config committed under `explainer_engine/training/pods/`; a scratchpad launcher is never used. | `tests/unit/test_reusable_first.py` |
| OR-22 | **Rule one: no system decision on an assumption** (creator 2026-10-26 01:05 Tbilisi: "Claude's first rule: never take system decisions by assuming something; agree every detail with me; also in auto mode"). A system decision (the base model, the LoRA and its checkpoint, the training data and captions, a training or inference setting, the test design and its variants, a rule's wording or number, the pipeline order, any spend) is taken only from the creator's own words. Every detail that his words do not settle is listed and put to him before it is used, also in auto mode and in autonomous runs; a guess is never filled in, and a found rule that disagrees with his newest words is reported and asked about. Reversible bookkeeping (a branch, a draft, a scratch file) is not a system decision. | `tests/unit/test_agree_every_detail.py` |
| OR-23 | **A detailed plan before every pod** (creator 2026-10-26 01:06 Tbilisi: "before every pod launch, show me a detailed plan, not a general one"). Before `cloud/runpod.py create` the creator gets, and agrees to, the pod's plan item by item: purpose and the question it answers; base model and LoRA (exact file and checkpoint); data (which files, how many, captions, trigger); every training or inference setting (steps, rank, alpha, learning rate, resolution, seeds, prompts or prompt variants); what is saved and sampled, when, and where on HF; the GPU, its price per hour, the time cap, the money cap and the ledger; what stops the pod; what he will see and when; what happens after. A general summary ("train the style, about $3") is not a plan. | `tests/unit/test_agree_every_detail.py` |
| OR-24 | **Important decisions go to a higher-model thread; its confirmation starts the pod** (creator 2026-10-26 12:49 Tbilisi: "before every important decision ask a separate thread on a higher Claude version: you are Sonnet and plan, the main plan decisions are taken by a separate Opus thread; the coordinator makes the information pass between threads, complete but without reasoning, so that it is not biased; when the separate thread confirms the plan, start the pod"). When that thread confirms the pod plan (every OR-23 item, inside the caps of `config/spend_caps.json`), the pod is started through `cloud/runpod.py create`: the confirmation takes the place of a separate creator yes for the launch. The creator's own words still decide a change of his goal, any spend above the plan or the caps, the RunPod top-up, and deleting anything. | `tests/unit/test_agree_every_detail.py` |
| OR-25 | **Direct evaluations, full-scale plans** (creator 2026-10-26 15:57 UTC, to the planning thread): an evaluation is always direct (what is good, what is bad, with the evidence, no softening); a plan is always full scale: it starts from the goal, lists every path to reach it, and grounds each path in known training methods and in the project's goals, not only the next step. | house (creator 2026-10-26) | review of every plan and every evaluation by the higher-model thread (OR-24) |
| OR-26 | **Opus plans, Sonnet works** (creator 2026-10-26 17:06 UTC): the working session (Sonnet) does the work (code, runs, collages, reports); the higher-model thread (Opus) writes the plans, and every hard technical question (code design, test design for a new method) goes to it before the work starts; the working session implements the plan as written and reports deviations instead of making them. | house (creator 2026-10-26) | the plan exists in a project file or thread before the code; test `test_agree_every_detail.py` |

## Series structure (SR-xx) for time-jump episodes ("what happens after a day, a week, a month, a year, 10 years, 100 years")

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| SR-01 | One video, one question the whole episode answers; the periods are its chapters. The final chapter compares the story with the real record ("would this group have outrun real history?"). |
| SR-02 | Periods do **not** get equal time. A period earns minutes by how many surprises it carries; a stretched period is cut. Length is a result, not a target. |
| SR-03 | Every period ends on a hook that is the question of the next period ("and a month later?"). The last chapter ends on the creator's open question about the next episode only if the creator set one. |
| SR-04 | **Science grounded, creative in the telling.** Established facts and models (historic population and technology growth, the knowledge-versus-materials problem, energy, tools, medicine) carry the story; each "what if" step says what is established, what is reasoned and what is a guess ([skill science-grounding](../.claude/skills/science-grounding/SKILL.md)) in the claim map and the description; the narration never reads a source out (BP-15). The counterfactual is a reasoned argument, not a prophecy. |
| SR-05 | Knowledge is not the only limit. Each period names its real bottleneck (people, materials, tools, food, disease, energy, trust), because the interesting argument is whether knowledge alone speeds progress. |
| SR-06 | After 100 years the original people are gone; what remains is their effect on the world (schools, written knowledge, tools, institutions, descendants) and it is shown. |
| SR-07 | Tone mix: mostly drama and curious telling with honest doubt, and humour in small doses: the jokes' density and placement are [BP-09](SOURCED_RULES.md) (at most one verbal joke a minute, the first early, beside the fact). Style: "scientific what-if". Humour never touches a fact (UR-21). |
| SR-08 | The script plans reuse: for each period it lists which pictures are unique, which repeat with a different crop or zoom, and which dialogue scenes share one picture with speech bubbles. Reuse is chosen when it fits the story, never to save money against it. |

## Shorts (SH-xx)

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| SH-01 | Shorts exist to advertise the video: a **hook of one question, spoken from the first second and complete by BP-11's hook time** (one statement for the `anti_intuition` and `pov_you` styles, SS-05 in [STORY_ENGINE](STORY_ENGINE.md)), then the most surprising scene inside the length bands and word budget of BP-12 (the hook timing is BP-11), then a line that points to the video. |
| SH-02 | A Short has its own text and script (`shorts` block of the production script); its voice is the episode's voice; pace may be raised by at most 8 % only when the timbre check passes, otherwise left as it is. |
| SH-03 | Pictures are the video's pictures, cropped to 9:16 around the named heroes and the action (the crop centre is set per shot); faces are never cut. |
| SH-04 | Each Short's opener is true and sourced by the claim map; its form is BP-11's (a question, or one statement that raises questions, SH-01). |
| SH-05 | **A set of Shorts tells different scenes.** For one video's set (up to 10 planned for the 8-12 min video) every Short opens its own question and tells its own scene: no two hooks alike, no body line told in two Shorts, no two Shorts on mostly the same pictures (PV-28). Which angle wins is measured per Short (viewed vs swiped away, [PUBLISHING_AND_ANALYTICS](PUBLISHING_AND_ANALYTICS.md)), never assumed. |

## Money ledger of a run
`jobs/<run>/run.json` holds caps, reservations, paid items and failures; the dollar and credit totals of the report are read from
it. Caps for the 2026-10-16 night run: Gemini US$6.00 new spend (pictures and judge), ElevenLabs credits up to 100,000 on the
account with a plan of about 25,000 for this episode, vidIQ up to 40 credits, RunPod none.
