C3. VIDEO: CONTINUITY BY CHAINING, NOT SEPARATE SCENES. The old rule generated every scene separately from its own picture; that was a big mistake. Consequences seen in the film: scenes simply change during a conversation; the heroes stand in different places and look different from shot to shot; there is no lead-in and no lead-out.
New principle: the next LTX shot is generated from a frame taken from the LAST SECONDS of the previous clip (the best frame of the tail, not necessarily the very last one), so the film flows from shot to shot; generation should be extended (longer continuous runs). Each shot must be COMPOSED so that its final frame is usable to continue the next video (end state planned in advance: who stands where, facing where, what the camera sees). Plan which shots need a fresh anchor picture (a new place or a time jump) and which shots continue a chain.

IMPORTANT, the creator's addition: the tail-frame chain is only the BASE IDEA, not a fixed rule. If you know better or additional ways to make the film continuous AND varied (for example: a new anchor picture at a deliberate cut, a reverse angle generated from the same scene, an insert / cutaway, a different camera angle of the same moment, a match cut, a time-jump anchor, first-and-last-frame style tricks you know of, or any other technique from current AI-video practice or filmmaking), extend and improve this idea. The goal is to DIVERSIFY the shots so the film does not look like one long identical chain. For each shot say which technique you chose and why, and list the alternatives you considered, including the ones you rejected.

C4. SCENE GRAMMAR: INTRODUCE, LET LINES FINISH, SHOW REACTIONS. A scene needs an introduction (an establishing / lead-in beat). After a scene, at least about half a second of picture must remain, and then EITHER we see the other hero's emotion, OR the camera crosses to the other side and the next scene or the narrator starts there.
