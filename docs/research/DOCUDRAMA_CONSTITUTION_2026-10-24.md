# Historical docudrama: the 15-minute structure (research, 2026-10-24)

**What this is.** ვანო asked for ten structures of one and the same 15-minute animated docudrama (narrator plus
characters who talk, shots of 3-5 s), differing only in structure, not content
([the ten variants](https://claude.ai/artifact/VMnW2tKfeTk8i4mkhy1NqZ)). He gave them to another AI that can reach
YouTube and brought back its report, "the storytelling constitution" (Georgian, 2026-10-24, kept by the creator). This
file is our English summary of that report and of what we checked. The rules it led to are template D and ST-17..ST-19 in
[STORY_ENGINE.md](../STORY_ENGINE.md), with the numbers in [BP-104 and BP-105](../SOURCED_RULES.md).

## The report's verdict on the ten variants

| Variant | Verdict | Why (the report) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 classic alternation (narrator <-> scene) | best micro-structure | the narrator carries context, the characters carry the action |
| 4 three acts (4 / 7 / 4 min) | best macro-structure | classic escalation: set-up, complications, one climax |
| 6 two storylines braided | conditional: wars and conflicts | the viewer sees both commanders in their own places |
| 8 question chapters | conditional: inventions, economics, mysteries | every scene ends on the next question |
| 5 frame story | conditional, a helper | an A/B test of the opening against a question opening |
| 2 scene first (23 % narrator) | rejected | not enough context |
| 3 narrator led (63-67 %) | rejected | 120 s of unbroken narration is an explainer, not a docudrama |
| 7 six equal chapters | rejected | artificial pace |
| 9 reverse chronology | rejected | the viewer must rebuild cause and effect alone |
| 10 three mini-episodes | rejected | cliffhangers inside 15 minutes break the immersion |

The synthesis: three acts at the macro level, narrator/scene alternation at the micro level, and from variant 8 a scene
that ends on a new question.

## The 16-segment timeline it proposes

900 s in 16 segments (hook, information gap, orientation, four scenes with bridges, the central scene, the turning
point, fallout, the answer, callback, outro). The timeline itself now lives in one place, template D of
[STORY_ENGINE.md](../STORY_ENGINE.md), with our one change: the report's segment 16 asks for a subscribe call, which BP-43
forbids, so it is a pointer to the next video.

Its rules beside the timeline: unbroken narration at most 60-90 s; a scene exists only when it holds a conflict or a
decision that changes the status quo (no expository dialogue); the narrator never voices what the picture shows; inside
a scene the camera, focus and faces change every 3-5 s while place and time stay; a 0.75-1.0 s pause after a hard fact;
narrator text never repeated in full on screen (Mayer's redundancy principle); music only for emotional accents; a
historical uncertainty is said out loud. Modules: a war switches on two storylines; a political or personal rivalry
lowers the narrator to about 25-30 %; an invention, money war or mystery uses question chapters with a eureka moment as
the turning point.

## What we checked, and what we did with it

- **Kept as template D** (hypotheses, REVIEW): the 16-segment timeline, narration blocks of at most 90 s (BP-104,
  ST-17), the central scene as the longest scene (ST-18), the narrator's share 25-45 % (BP-105, ST-19).
- **Already our rules, so linked, not copied:** the hook is the first sentence (BP-03); no subscribe or goodbye at the
  end (BP-43; the report's segment 16 is replaced); a hold is never over 1 s (BP-10, which the 0.75-1.0 s pause fits);
  narration that describes the picture (KP-20); every scene turns (SC-02) and wants collide (SC-03); one dramatic
  question answered at the end (ST-01); music spotted, not wallpaper (BP-62).
- **Captions:** the style bible keeps captions on every video as a closed-caption track; the long video burns no full
  narrator subtitles into the picture (redundancy). Shorts keep their burned captions (BP-13).
- **Not verified by us, not encoded:** "the first 30 s decide 65-84 % of viewers", the effect sizes it quotes for
  Mayer's principles (d = -0.36, d = 1.30), and the view counts of the channels it studied. The report itself says
  that no study fixes the narrator/dialogue split and that YouTube does not publish other channels' retention.
- **Its "sources unreachable" note is outdated for us:** we timed The Food That Built America S1E1 and Roman Empire S1E1
  from their subtitles earlier the same day ([the timed tables](https://claude.ai/artifact/8LHahUHh45NHZfSzo2fVSo)).
- **Its test plan** (dips during dialogue -> shorter scenes; rewinds on maps -> longer pauses; an A/B test of a frame
  opening against a question opening, adopted only after three videos agree) is how BP-104 and BP-105 move.
