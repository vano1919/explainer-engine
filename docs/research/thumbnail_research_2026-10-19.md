# Thumbnail and packaging research (2026-10-19)

Requested by the creator ("this is not a simple matter"). Five web-research passes with WebSearch/WebFetch only; no vidIQ
credit and no paid call was used. Every URL was accessed 2026-10-19. The fetch tool returns small-model summaries, so quotes
are as returned, not byte-checked; re-read a page before quoting it publicly. Tags: [E] first-hand official page or peer-reviewed,
[V] vendor study (observational, no control group), [P] practitioner opinion, [U] unverified (snippet or secondary).
The rules derived from this are in [THUMBNAIL_RULES](../THUMBNAIL_RULES.md).

## What the research settles, and what it does not

1. **Policy and platform facts are solid** (Part 1, Part 5 of the appendices): what is forbidden, how Test & Compare judges
   (watch time share, not clicks), what CTR YouTube calls typical (half of channels 2-10 %), how impressions are counted.
2. **Element rules are weak.** No randomized public dataset isolates one thumbnail element. The large observational studies
   (Eden: 507,478 videos; vidIQ: 500 breakouts) find small or no differences for face, colour and brightness; the only gaps just
   above their noise level are 4-6 words of text (+4.1 points) and eye contact (+3.2 points). Vendor "+20-40 % CTR" tables carry
   no method. Consequence: our element rules are labelled **hypotheses to test on our own channel**, not laws.
3. **No evidence either way** for the things this channel does: illustrated/cartoon thumbnails, mystery silhouettes,
   a recurring motif, arrows, curiosity-gap imagery. The only support is general theory (information gap, Loewenstein 1994;
   distinctive brand assets, Romaniuk 2018) and one meta-analysis of headlines (Le Quere & Matias 2025: a teaser works best
   with a concrete anchor plus one withheld piece; pure vagueness does not reliably win).
4. **Niche case studies could not be verified** (YouTube pages are not readable by the tool; no thumbnail was seen). What exists:
   a vendor page describing one shared template of three fast-growing faceless 2D channels (plain light background, one waist-up
   character in the left third, 1-2 words in the right third, one small arrow, readable at 168 x 94 px) and one vendor case where
   removing horror-style faces, red arrows and clickbait words did not lower CTR (5.6 % to 6.4 %, confounded). Treat both as hypotheses.
5. **The sexual-content line is explicit** (Appendix A, "Sexual / suggestive content"): body parts as the focal point, or a pose or
   clothing intended to arouse, are what YouTube names; illustrated and animated content has no exemption; the cost of a
   mistake is age restriction, removal, or limited/no ads. A clothed noir silhouette is workable inside it; the rule in
   THUMBNAIL_RULES keeps a wide margin.
6. **Title, tags and description**: tags "play a minimal role" per YouTube Help; the title carries the written promise, the thumbnail the
   visual one, the first description line repeats the keyword, the first seconds pay the promise off (vidIQ; YouTube has no official
   "packaging" text).

## Open gaps (what would still need data)

- Real thumbnails of animated history/crime/game-lore channels with their numbers: needs either the creator's own look at the
  top videos of a few channels, or a vidIQ outlier / similar-thumbnail session (paid credits, needs the creator's OK).
- Whether a recurring silhouette helps recognition on YouTube: only our own A/B tests can say.
- Display pixel sizes on phone, TV and sidebar: no official page found; "168 x 94" is a community stress test.
- Primary text of Loewenstein 1994, Molina CHI 2021, the Creator Insider post on CTR and watch time, 1of10's 2025 report (403).


# Appendix A. Policy line, title, tags, description, Shorts

### Sexual / suggestive content
- Thumbnails policy https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9229980 : prohibited "Pornographic imagery"; "Sexual acts, the use of sex toys, fetishes, or other sexually gratifying imagery"; "Nudity, including genitals"; "Imagery that depicts unwanted sexualization". Borderline judged by: "Whether breasts, buttocks, or genitals are the focal point of the thumbnail"; "Whether the subject is depicted in a pose or clothing that is intended to sexually arouse the viewer". Consequences: porn -> channel termination; borderline -> age-restricted or removed, no strike; others removal + possible strike; 3 strikes/90 days -> termination.
- Nudity and sexual content https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2802002 : "Explicit content meant to be sexually gratifying is not allowed"; "clothed or unclothed genitals, breasts, or buttocks that are meant for sexual gratification" not allowed (clothing does not make safe); provocative dancing focused on genitals/buttocks/breasts prohibited; age-restriction for other sexual content (hidden from under-18s and signed-out viewers). Page does not use "sexually suggestive". Covers "real-world, dramatized, illustrated, and animated content" (quote cut, partly [unverified]).
- Advertiser-friendly, Adult content https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278 : sexually suggestive = "visual, audible, or written-out undertones implying intent to sexually arouse an audience". Limited ads: "Titles or thumbnails with sexualized themes (including misleading signals)". No ads: "a video thumbnail with sexual content (including texts or links)"; titles/thumbnails referencing 18+/21+; "Thumbnails intending to mislead viewers with sexualized depictions of normal objects or scenes"; "Focal, implicit sexual act or behavior". Applies to video, Short, thumbnail, title, description, tags. "This policy includes both real and computer-generated visuals." Animated: "Non-fleeting depictions of nudity (animated, real-life, or dramatized)" no ads. No cartoon exemption.
- Gemini: API terms (ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms) -> Prohibited Use Policy; Generative AI use policy (policies.google.com/terms/generative-ai/use-policy) lists "Sexually explicit content ... for the purpose of pornography or sexual gratification" (3.4); safety settings page has sexually_explicit category; image-model filter strictness on suggestive-but-not-explicit imagery NOT confirmed [unverified].
- Practical line (derived from wording): AVOID breasts/buttocks/groin as focal point; pose or clothing intended to arouse (arched back, hip thrust, spread legs, bent toward camera); nudity or nudity-implying forms (skin-coloured silhouette without clothing lines); provocative dance; sexual text, 18+/21+; sexualised image that misleads about content. SAFER: fully clothed figure from shoulders/waist up or neutral full-length stance; noir carried by hat, smoke, shadow, venetian-blind light, cigarette; face/profile as focus not torso/hips. Risk surface = the thumbnail; "intended to arouse" judged by reviewers, keep a clear margin; "sexualized themes" alone = limited ads.

### Title

Snippets [unverified]: 50-70 chars (about 60 mobile, 70 search); one 2026 study 30-50 (air.io). Conflict: "front-load, then about 50-70". Keyword first when natural. Numbers add specificity, not in every title. Caps: one emphasised word; all caps harder to scan/spammy. Thumbnail match: "Avoid repeating the same message in both places"; title adds context. One strong emotional word. "Curiosity is fine. A broken promise is not." Title Flip article (curiosity title first, keyword title after first wave, watch Studio 48 h) [unverified].

# Appendix B. What YouTube itself says

### 1. CTR vs watch time, satisfaction
- Help "Impressions and click-through rate FAQ" https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154 [fetched]: CTR "measures how often viewers watched a video after seeing a registered impression"; "likely represents a subset of your channel's total views". Clickbait thumbnails often have high CTR but low average view duration, making them less likely to be recommended (summary wording; re-read before quoting).
- YouTube Blog, Cristos Goodrow, "On YouTube's recommendation system", 15 Sept 2021 https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/ [fetched]: "Clicking on a video provides a strong indication that you will also find it satisfying."; "But we learned back in 2011 that clicking on a video doesn't mean you actually watched it."; "When we first incorporated watchtime into recommendations, we saw an immediate 20% drop in views."; valued watchtime measured by user surveys, 1-5 stars, only 4-5 count; Wimbledon 2011 example (thumbnail/title suggest match footage, video was a person talking); 2015 "sensationalistic tabloid content" demoted. Post doesn't use "thumbnail"/"clickbait" as guidance (reading).
- How YouTube Works / recommendations: satisfaction surveys help recommendations; nothing on CTR/thumbnails.
- Creator Insider on X, 2024-12 (id 1869454676917424246) snippet only [unverified]: "Watch time provides both CTR ... and retention ... (Focusing on CTR can lead to clickbait which can tank retention which can tank performance)". Todd Beaupre / Rene Ritchie claims: secondhand, do not cite. Matt Koval: nothing found. "Promise and payoff": not in any official source (community vocabulary).
- Studio blog "Better insights" 7 Mar 2018 https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-studio-better-insights-new/ : impression counted when a viewer sees a thumbnail; "Effective thumbnails and titles that attract your target audience may drive this rate up."

### 2. Test & Compare (Help 13861714, 16391400) [fetched]
Up to 3 variants (title only, thumbnail only, or both); winner = highest watch time; small control group sees only default and is excluded; "completed within two weeks" (a few days to 2 weeks); no numeric traffic minimum (low impressions can prevent a winner; diverse variants and more impressions shorten tests; very similar variants longer); labels Winner / Performed Same / Inconclusive (inconclusive: first upload stays default; older page snippet also lists Preferred / None [partly unverified]); desktop only in Studio with advanced features; not Shorts, Scheduled Lives, Premieres (eligible after conversion), made-for-kids, mature-audience or private; changing title/thumbnail mid-test stops it. Official guidance: test older videos first; clearly different variants; upload 1280x720 or higher (any under 720p -> all downscaled to 480p).

### 3. Benchmarks / Studio
- 7628154: "Half of all channels and videos on YouTube have an impressions CTR that can range between 2% and 10%."; varies by content, audience, impression location; differences "might be due to traffic sources"; new videos/channels (<1 week) or <100 views have wider range; compare long-term.
- Traffic source https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300 : check CTR on Home and Suggested in first 24 h for general audiences; Subscriptions feed CTR for subscribers. No numeric per-source benchmark.
- Impression https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7577430 : counted when thumbnail shown > 1 s and >= 50 % visible; counted on YouTube apps/TV/consoles, Search, home, feeds, Up Next, playlists; NOT counted: external sites/embeds, mobile website, YouTube Kids, Music, cards/end screens, email/notifications, background tab, TrueView discovery ads. Impressions CTR is a subset of views, not comparable across channels with different traffic mix.

### 4. Specs and policy
- https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431 : 3840x2160 recommended for video (2160x3840 Shorts), min width 640 px; JPG/PNG; 50 MB desktop, 2 MB mobile; 16:9 / 9:16; custom thumbnails need a verified account; must follow Community Guidelines; repeat offences may remove custom-thumbnail privileges for 30 days. Pixel display sizes on feeds/TV: no official page found.
- Spam policy https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973 "Malicious clickbait": "Using maliciously misleading titles, thumbnails, descriptions, or imagery to trick users into clicking" - bar is "maliciously".
- Monetisation https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392 : "Generic or Repetitive Content" renamed "inauthentic content" 15 July 2025; reviewers may assess "Video metadata (including titles, thumbnails, and descriptions)"; template-looking content covered; reused content judged channel-wide. No official wording on AI thumbnails.

### 5. Overpromise/satisfaction
Policy: malicious clickbait rule; ranking: high CTR + low average view duration less likely recommended (7628154); satisfaction: Goodrow 1-5 star surveys. No official numeric weights anywhere; "clickbait boosts CTR 40-60 %" claims are unsourced.

# Appendix C. Element-by-element evidence
Headline: NO randomized, public, multi-channel A/B dataset isolates single thumbnail elements. Everything is (a) observational "what do winners share" data, (b) vendor blog numbers with no method, or (c) YouTube's own docs. Nothing found is peer-reviewed on CTR.

Sources: S1 Eden https://eden.so/blog/what-makes-a-good-youtube-thumbnail/ (vendor; 507,478 long-form videos, 8,691 channels, Aug 2025-Aug 2026; breakout = >= 2x channel median, 33.7 %; underperformer = at/below baseline, 42.1 %; 2,960 thumbnails analysed, 1,486 vs 1,474; vision model + partial hand check; gaps under ~2.5 points treated as noise; index skews to active growing channels; thumbnail/title/topic confounded). S2 vidIQ https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-thumbnail-study/ (vendor; 500 long-form breakouts, 30 niches, month to 2026-06-30; shares among winners only, no control group). S3 1of10 via Search Engine Journal https://www.searchenginejournal.com/do-faces-help-youtube-thumbnails-heres-what-the-data-says/563944/ (vendor; > 300,000 viral 2025 videos; SEJ gives no numbers; primary report returned 403). S4 Thumbnail Bench via Net Influencer https://www.netinfluencer.com/?p=53558 (vendor, snippet only; 1,043 channels 100k+ subs, 11,223 thumbnails; prevalence only). S5 Kapwing 2024 emotion study (vendor, snippet; prevalence). S6 mer.vin compilation (Sept 2026; aggregates CTRpilot/ThumbMentor/"127 A/B tests", no method, internally inconsistent) [unverified]. S7 YouTube Help Test & Compare (official). S8 academic: Yangandul et al. ACM ETRA 2018 eye tracking (snippet: ~2x attention to thumbnails vs title text, looked at first); Weismueller & Harrigan 2021 (50,000 thumbnails; only abstract seen); arXiv 2410.19825 (no CTR data). S9 Veritasium "We Need To Talk About Clickbait" 2021 via gigazine (anecdotes, confounded).
Not found: any published ThumbnailTest, TubeBuddy, Spotter, Paddy Galloway, Think Media, Colin & Samir dataset with numbers.

Per element:
- Face vs no face: Eden face in 84.2 % of breakouts vs 82.2 % of underperformers (inside noise: no detectable association). 1of10: faces vs no faces "perform similarly" overall; a face helped only above an unstated subscriber threshold, modest lift; finance better with faces, business worse (no figures). vidIQ: face in 69 % of breakouts (75 % of top 100, 80 % of top 50) but no baseline; Thumbnail Bench: faces on 71 % of covers from 100k+ channels, so 69 % is no higher than general prevalence. Blog claims "+33 % (6.4 % vs 4.8 %)" have no method [unverified]. Disagreement: vidIQ frames faces as mattering most; Eden and 1of10 find about no difference; survivorship bias is central.
- Emotion/exaggerated expression: vidIQ ~5 % of breakouts exaggerated; "a clear face matters more than an extreme expression" (conclusion not experiment). Eden shocked expression 11.7 % of breakouts from >100k channels vs 5.6 % smaller. Prevalence of "shocked" ranges 5-27 % depending on source/classifier; none shows it causes clicks.
- Number of faces: 1of10 (via SEJ) multiple faces did better than single, no numbers [unverified].
- Eye contact: Eden 32.4 % of breakouts vs 29.2 % underperformers (3.2 points, just above noise). Gaze toward an object: no test found.
- Text: Eden any text 86.6 % vs 83.2 %; 4-6 words 30.6 % vs 26.5 % (biggest gap in the study, 4.1 points); 7+ words slightly more common among underperformers; breakouts average ~6.8 words. vidIQ: median 5 words where text present; most common pattern is NO text (28 %), then 1-3 words (24 %). Mild disagreement between sources. Font size: no data (blog rules unsourced).
- Text repeating title: Eden verbatim repetition rare in both groups (6.3 %): no signal.
- Colour: Eden pixel-measured brightness 40.9 vs 40.5, contrast 23.5 vs 23.1, saturation 35.2 vs 34.7, warm share 31.1 vs 30.5: no measurable difference. vidIQ high contrast in ~56 % of breakouts, 46 % in top 50: "baseline, not differentiator". S6 vendor claims (contrast >= 7:1 +38 %, warm vs cool, muted -47 %) have no sample sizes or design [unverified] and are contradicted by the two measured datasets.
- Dark thumbnails: Eden no difference; no dark-thumbnail experiment found.
- Composition/subject size/background: vidIQ describes winners' face ~ a third of frame, strong contrast, few words (description, not test). S6 tight-crop claim unverified. Rule of thirds: no data.
- Arrows/circles, before/after, borders, logos, numbers, curiosity-gap imagery, mystery silhouettes, illustrated vs photo: NO data-backed source found for any. For an illustrated, mystery-heavy channel this is a genuine evidence gap.
- Curiosity/clickbait: Veritasium anecdote only.
Small vs large channels: only direct evidence Eden's shocked-expression split and 1of10's unstated subscriber threshold; Thumbnail Bench sample 100k+ only; blog CTR-by-size tables unsourced. No source splits effects by browse/search/suggested. Effects from big-channel samples may not transfer to a <10k channel.
Mobile legibility: "168x94" repeated across blogs but sources disagree what surface it is; a design stress test, not a documented YouTube spec; eye tracking (ETRA 2018) supports only that thumbnails get first and strongest attention.
What the evidence supports: metric choice matters (Test & Compare ranks by watch time share, so a high-CTR thumbnail can lose); large observational samples find small or no differences for face, colour, brightness; the only gaps just above noise are 4-6 words of text (4.1 pts) and eye contact (3.2 pts), both in one vendor study. Honest conclusion: the only trustworthy method is your own Test & Compare on your own channel.

### 1. Curiosity gap, clickbait
- [E] Loewenstein 1994, "The Psychology of Curiosity", Psychological Bulletin 116(1):75-98: curiosity = deprivation from a perceived gap in knowledge; needs some prior knowledge (no knowledge, no gap). https://stafforini.com/works/loewenstein-1994-psychology-curiosity-review/ [U: snippet]. "Closeable / not too big or small" = popular summaries [P] (pbs.org/wgbh/nova/article/how-to-stimulate-curiosity/).
- [E] Le Quere & Matias, Scientific Reports Jan 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-81575-9, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11704130/ : meta-analysis of 8,977 headline experiments; concreteness vs CTR is curvilinear: too-vague baseline -> more concreteness raises CTR; already-concrete -> more concreteness lowers CTR. Reading: concrete anchor (named hero/event) + one withheld piece.
- [E] Molina, Sundar et al., CHI 2021 "Does clickbait actually attract more clicks?" (abstract snippet): clickbait-classified headlines did not produce more curiosity; detectors agree on 47 %; mixed engagement. https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/does-clickbait-actually-atract-more-clicks-three-clickbait-studie/ [U].
- No primary YouTube data on clickbait harming retention/subscribers; claims are [P]/[U]. YouTube Test & Compare picks winners by watch time share "because titles and thumbnails should help viewers get engagement from the right videos" [E, Help 16391400]. A Todd Beaupre quote found second-hand only: do not cite.
- Policy https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973 : "Using maliciously misleading titles, thumbnails, descriptions, or imagery to trick users"; example: title offers full sports match, video shows only a clip; consequence monetisation loss/termination. (fetch paraphrased; re-check exact wording). Line (inference): a teaser is fine if the video delivers exactly what the packaging promises.

### 2. Colour and salience
- [E, limited] KAIST eye-tracking (icon displays): warm colours more eye-catching; highly saturated colours drew most attention against black. https://koasas.kaist.ac.kr/handle/10203/200596
- [E] Digital-platform eye-tracking 2025: warm = more attention/arousal, cool = more concentration. https://research.nottingham.edu.cn/en/publications/impact-of-color-hues-on-arousal-concentration-and-visual-attentio/
- 200,000+ exposures of 423 book covers: warm/cool vs neutral raised CTR, effect varied by theme (core.ac.uk/works/83384105) [snippet, U].
- Student study: saturated ads preferred, warm marginally; stated preference not clicks.
- Folklore [P/U]: "colour raises CTR 20-40 %" untraceable; complementary-colour rules and dark-mode-feed rules have no primary evidence.
- Working rules (inference): high luminance and saturation contrast against feed background; one dominant warm accent; verify by own A/B.

### 3. Generative-AI thumbnails
- YouTube Help 14328491: disclosure for realistic content that makes a real person say/do something they didn't, alters real footage, realistic scene that didn't occur; not for non-realistic/animation; AI help for outline/script/thumbnail/title = production assistance, no disclosure. A thumbnail showing a realistic fake scene falls under deception policy. Hand-drawn style: disclosure not triggered (reading; creator confirms).
- Gemini image generation https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation : text rendering presented as strength; third-party reports say Nano Banana 2 handles short text better but imperfect on long/small text [U]; every output carries SynthID. References: Nano Banana 2.1 / Gemini 3.1 Flash Image: up to 5 character + 10 object refs, no style refs; 3.1 Flash Lite Image: up to 4 character refs, 1K only; Gemini 3 Pro Image: no character refs, up to 3 style refs. (Note: checks against our `imagegen/routing.py` premium tier = gemini-3-pro-image.) Hands/uncanny faces: anecdotes only.
- Why composite headline separately (inference): exact spelling, one typeface across episodes, legibility at thumbnail size.
- Viewer reaction to AI-looking thumbnails: no CTR/survey data; [P] Uppbeat blog: "missing intent". Adjacent [E]: AI profile pictures rated highly but disclosure of AI origin lowered ratings (profile photos, not thumbnails).
- Recurring-character prompt structure [P]: attach reference, "same character as the reference, silhouette and outfit unchanged", anchor traits in words, scene described separately, name palette/background/aspect, "no text", character sheet as reference. No official Google template; "93 % consistency" claims unsourced.

### 4. A/B testing, small channel
- Test & Compare (Help 16391400, 13861714) [E]: up to 3 variants; title, thumbnail or both; winner = watch time share not CTR; desktop Studio; ineligible: Shorts, Premieres, scheduled lives, kids, private; concurrent, "a few days up to two weeks"; labels Winner (statistically significant) / Performed Same / Inconclusive (inconclusive keeps first-uploaded variant); editing title/thumbnail mid-test stops it; very similar variants and low impressions make a winner unlikely; thumbnails under 1280x720 downscaled to 480p; small control group held out; confound named by YouTube: early impressions skew to people who know the channel; third-party tools often sequential and CTR-optimised; test older videos first; split/significance method unpublished.
- Manual before/after weak [P]: traffic source, weekday, age change; judge on browse/home CTR not overall.
- z-test (two-proportion): assumes independent impressions; unreliable under ~10 clicks per arm [P].
- Worked example (computed): alpha 0.05 two-sided, power 80 %; n per arm = (1.96*sqrt(2*pbar*(1-pbar)) + 0.8416*sqrt(p1(1-p1)+p2(1-p2)))^2/(p2-p1)^2. Baseline 4.0 %, +20 % relative (4.8 %): ~10,300 impressions per variant (~20,600 for 2 arms, ~30,900 for 3). Sensitivity at 4 %: +10 % ~39,500/arm; +30 % ~4,800; +50 % ~1,900. Rule of thumb n ~ 16*p(1-p)/delta^2 (~9,600). Small channels can only detect large lifts (+30-50 % relative): test bold differences. YouTube judges on watch time share, so this CTR calculation is a planning aid only.

### 5. Recurring brand motif
- [E via secondary] Romaniuk, "Building Distinctive Brand Assets" (2018): distinctive assets (colours, logos, mascots, sounds) trigger recall; judged on fame and uniqueness; building fame takes sustained consistency; marketers' gut judgement of asset strength rarely accurate (Ehrenberg-Bass, Journal of Brand Management). https://www.marketingweek.com/marketers-judgement-strength-brand-assets-rarely-accurate/ , https://marketingscience.info/brands-need-distinctive-assets/ (primary not seen).
- No YouTube-thumbnail-specific study. Stable silhouette/palette/type slot = reasoned application of brand-asset theory, not YouTube-tested. Treat motif as hypothesis; A/B (motif vs none, same image otherwise).
