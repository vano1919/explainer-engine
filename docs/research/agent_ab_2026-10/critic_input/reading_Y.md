## S01 the fog on the Moscow road
- NARRATOR: The drums stop first.
- NARRATOR: It's the nineteenth of October, 1812, and the army is leaving Moscow, a city that burned around it.
- NARRATOR: Then a fog drops on the road. Wagons vanish. Where the road was, there's cold grass up to the knee.
- NARRATOR: When the fog thins, you're standing in the year 1223. That's five hundred and eighty-nine years back.
- NARRATOR: Napoleon has an army and one plan: find whoever lives here, and break them in a single day.
- NARRATOR: But the people who live here have a plan of their own. Can he walk this army out alive?
## S02 the riders on every ridge
- NARRATOR: By noon the fog is gone, and riders sit on every ridge, still as fence posts. Napoleon wants a battle. Aslan, a scout of the Mameluke company, has just ridden back from them. You can read it on his face.
- napoleon: Where is their army?
- aslan: These are all I found, sire. Riders. When I rode closer, they rode back.
- napoleon: Then we ride faster. The wagons keep up.
- aslan: The wagons are sinking to the axle, sire. This grass is not a road.
- napoleon: Then they follow. Count the guns tonight.
- aslan: Sire. I understand their shouts. It's my grandmother's tongue. Cuman. A tongue that died.
- napoleon: Dead?
- napoleon: Murat rides at first light. Whatever they are, I will find their army.
- NARRATOR: He doesn't ask what year it is, though you would. He sends for Murat. But the riders on the ridge are already waiting.
## S03 Murat's charge
- NARRATOR: Murat takes the cavalry out at first light, plumes first. The riders on the ridge turn and go.
- NARRATOR: He chases. They hold the same distance, and one twists in the saddle to shoot. Their arrows carry four hundred metres.
- NARRATOR: Every rider has three or four horses and swaps without slowing. Murat has one, and rides it until it falls. You'd think he'd look back. He doesn't.
- NARRATOR: By dusk the best cavalry in the army is walking home. Nobody was caught. Nobody was fought.
- NARRATOR: Napoleon wanted a battle he could win in a day. The riders took the day. Now the heavy wagons, miles behind, are sinking into the grass.
## S04 the khan's tent
- NARRATOR: Aslan was the last man off that field. Two riders took him up the ridge to a felt tent. A man by the fire said nothing for a long time, and you can feel every minute of it.
- NARRATOR: That man is Genghis Khan, and he has two tumens on this grass, ten thousand men each. He speaks Mongol, a Cuman scout turns it into Kipchak, and Aslan understands.
- genghis: How far do the long iron tubes throw?
- aslan: Far enough to trouble you, great khan.
- genghis: Your emperor hides behind wagons. His tubes throw as well as his wagons move.
- aslan: Nine hundred metres. Further than any arrow you own.
- genghis: Good. That is all I asked.
- aslan: You tricked me.
- genghis: Jebe shot my horse, once. I asked who did it. He said, I did.
- aslan: And you killed him?
- genghis: I kept him. Honest men are rare. Be one, Aslan.
- aslan: I'm the emperor's rider, great khan. Not a gift.
- NARRATOR: At dawn they send Aslan back, unharmed. The khan knows how far the guns shoot. Now he wants to watch them do it.
## S05 the valley and the guns
- NARRATOR: Napoleon trained as an artillery officer, and you can tell. He finds his valley by noon. Guns on the rim, squares below.
- NARRATOR: The riders come at a gallop. At four hundred metres their arrows start dropping among the squares, and you can hear every one.
- NARRATOR: The guns answer at nine hundred. Round shot tears lanes through the horse lines.
- NARRATOR: The few that reach the squares meet silence, then one volley. A steady square is rarely broken by cavalry alone.
- NARRATOR: The riders wheel away. The army cheers. But nobody asks why they gave up so easily.
## S06 the wagons burn
- NARRATOR: The cheering stops at midnight, when a rider staggers in from the rear without his horse. The wagon train is burning. The army's food rested on those wagons, and you can't forage this grass.
- NARRATOR: Napoleon rides back to the ashes with Murat and Aslan. At the edge of the firelight, a Cuman rider waits with one hand raised. You know that gesture in any tongue.
- napoleon: We march at dawn. They have an army somewhere. Now I will have it.
- aslan: Sire, the Cuman rider asks you to a parley.
- napoleon: A man who burns my wagons at night does not talk.
- aslan: He let me walk home from his tent, sire. Nobody followed me.
- napoleon: Murat. How many rounds are left for each gun?
- napoleon: What does he want, Aslan?
- aslan: He didn't say, sire. He named the hour. Sunrise.
- napoleon: Right. Then we go and hear it.
- NARRATOR: He has never asked an enemy what he wants. At sunrise he rides out to ask. But you can guess the answer will cost him.
## S07 the parley
- NARRATOR: Open ground, a felt carpet, two stools. Genghis Khan has brought his two tumens. Napoleon brings Aslan. Every word crosses three tongues, and you'd need Aslan's grandmother's to follow it.
- napoleon: Name your price. My army is leaving.
- genghis: You came without your wagons. Does the road feel long?
- napoleon: Longer than your patience. Name it.
- genghis: Two guns stay on this grass. And the men who fire them.
- aslan: Sire, a gun takes fifteen men. He means thirty.
- napoleon: The guns, yes. French soldiers are not for sale.
- genghis: A man shot my horse once. He said so. He leads my right hand now.
- napoleon: I have no archers to promote. Only soldiers.
- aslan: Great khan, the guns stay. The men go home. Say yes.
- genghis: Good. A man who speaks when it counts.
- napoleon: What did you tell him?
- aslan: The price, sire. Exactly.
- NARRATOR: The khan nods once. Two guns are unhitched and left on the grass. Napoleon doesn't turn around. Now they only have to find the fog again.
## S08 the fog again
- NARRATOR: At dawn the fog comes back. The army walks into it without a word.
- NARRATOR: Behind it, two guns stand on the grass where the carpet was. A man in a felt hat walks round one, slowly. He lays a hand on the barrel, the way you'd test a good horse.
- NARRATOR: On the far side the road is Russian again. A clerk counts the guns, the way he does every night. Two are missing.
- NARRATOR: Napoleon never got his great battle. You'd call it a retreat, but he got his army out alive, and it cost him two guns.
- NARRATOR: Then the drums start again.
