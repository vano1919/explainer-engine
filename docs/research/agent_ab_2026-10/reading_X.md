## S01 The fog opens on the 1812 column and the drums go quiet on grass that is 589 years too early.
- NARRATOR: The drums stop first.
- NARRATOR: Then the fog lifts, and the whole column stands in grass that belongs to another century.
- NARRATOR: Not decades off. Five hundred and eighty-nine years early.
- NARRATOR: Napoleon came for one great battle. Who out here is going to give it to him?
## S02 A tired column with food for days meets an enemy who wins by never standing still; the narrator lays out the two ways of war.
- NARRATOR: Napoleon crossed the Neman with 450,000 men. Moscow saw about 100,000 of them.
- NARRATOR: He's a gunner at heart. Find the enemy's army, break it in one battle, go home.
- NARRATOR: But his wagons can't follow these tracks, so the column eats what it grabs along the road.
- NARRATOR: Out here, nobody grabs anything for free. Every Mongol rider keeps three or four horses and swaps mounts as he goes.
- NARRATOR: And Genghis Khan once kept the archer who shot his horse. The man owned up to it.
- NARRATOR: So what does a man who needs one battle do with an enemy who never needs to stand?
## S03 Napoleon wants Murat's cavalry loosed after riders who run too neatly; Ali, who knows how steppe riders flee, says the runners are leading them on.
- NARRATOR: Murat's riders are chasing a handful of runners, on horses he never bothers to count.
- NARRATOR: On the far ridge, one rider sits still and watches.
- NARRATOR: Napoleon wants the second wave loose before dark, and Ali, his Mameluke interpreter, stands at his stirrup.
- napoleon: Murat has them. Send the second wave.
- ali: Sire, with respect, they run slowly.
- napoleon: Slow or fast, they run. That is enough.
- ali: The shout from the ridge, Sire. It is near my mother's tongue. It says, come on.
- napoleon: Everyone shouts in a retreat.
- ali: Look at the dust behind the ridge. They are leading Murat on.
- napoleon: Then recall him.
- ali: The trumpets will not carry, Sire. He is over the crest.
- napoleon: Then the wing follows him. Bring up the guns.
- NARRATOR: At the Kalka, the Rus princes followed a retreat for nine days before it turned on them.
- NARRATOR: Murat's wing has until dark.
## S04 Napoleon turns to what he trusts, the guns and the squares, and the valley battle breaks the first wave; the army believes it has won.
- NARRATOR: Napoleon stops guessing and starts measuring. His twelve-pounders reach nine hundred meters.
- NARRATOR: The Mongol bow tops out near four hundred. A French musket, ninety.
- napoleon: Guns forward! Form squares!
- NARRATOR: A steady square holds its fire until the horsemen are close, and cavalry alone rarely breaks one.
- NARRATOR: Down the valley comes a tumen, ten thousand riders, straight into the guns.
- napoleon: Hold your fire! Wait for them!
- NARRATOR: They never get close. The first wave comes apart under the round shot, and the survivors turn and ride.
- napoleon: Fire!
- NARRATOR: By afternoon the valley belongs to the army. Men cheer, and the guns stay hot.
- NARRATOR: The Emperor has the battle he marched here for. So he trusts his guns even more.
## S05 At dusk the tumens do not return to the field: they ride round the victory and burn the wagons; the rider on the ridge is Genghis Khan.
- NARRATOR: Dusk. The tumens do not come back to the field.
- NARRATOR: They ride around the victory instead, two tumens, twenty thousand riders, and not one of them stops to fight.
- NARRATOR: Torches go into the wagon canvas. By midnight the bread is smoke.
- NARRATOR: The men who cheered at noon sit watching their supper burn, and the foraging road they live on now belongs to the riders.
- NARRATOR: On the far ridge, the rider who watched all day lifts one hand. That's Genghis Khan himself.
- NARRATOR: He never needed to win the Emperor's battle. So what does he want, when he finally speaks?
## S06 Under a truce sign Napoleon demands his battle at dawn; the khan, who never answers the challenge, asks only how far the long guns shoot.
- NARRATOR: A white cloth goes up on a lance at the edge of the burnt wagon line.
- NARRATOR: Napoleon rides out with Ali at his stirrup. The khan waits on a felt rug, in no hurry at all.
- napoleon: Dawn. Open ground. My army against yours.
- ali: He asks for a battle at dawn, Great Khan. On open ground.
- genghis: The grass is wide. Tell him to sit.
- napoleon: I do not sit. Repeat my terms.
- ali: He stands, Great Khan. He says the terms are not for sitting.
- napoleon: Alexander gave me five weeks of silence. You, at least, came to the table.
- genghis: Hmm, ask him how far the long guns shoot.
- ali: Sire, the khan asks how far the long guns reach.
- napoleon: Nine hundred meters, round shot. Six hundred, canister. Fifteen men to a gun.
- NARRATOR: The night turns cold on the burnt wagon line. By morning, the khan will name his price.
## S07 At dawn the khan names two guns as the price of the road; Ali softens the Emperor's insult, the khan hears the tone, and Ali confesses.
- NARRATOR: Dawn. Napoleon, the khan and Ali stand with two iron guns between them, and the fog at the valley's far end is thinning. When the sun burns it off, the way home closes.
- genghis: Tell him my price. The long guns. All of them.
- ali: Sire, the khan asks for the guns. For the road.
- napoleon: Tell this horse thief a gunner gives up his guns when he is dead.
- ali: Well, he says a gunner is fond of his guns, Great Khan.
- genghis: Your voice dropped. Say it again, as he said it.
- ali: Your tongue is near my mother's. I hear what you hear. He called you a horse thief.
- genghis: I kept an archer who shot my horse and told me so. Tell me true, always.
- genghis: Two guns, not all. Let him choose.
- napoleon: Take the two on the left. Quickly.
- NARRATOR: The sun climbs. Two guns will stay on the grass, and the khan's hand is already moving toward the first barrel.
## S08 The fog returns, the column walks out without two guns, and the khan's hand rests on a French barrel: no battle ended this war.
- NARRATOR: The fog rolls back in, and the drums pick up again, soft as rain.
- NARRATOR: The column walks into it without two guns.
- NARRATOR: Behind them, the khan's hand rests on a French barrel.
- NARRATOR: Napoleon got his battle. It ended nothing.
- NARRATOR: More than four hundred thousand men crossed one river for a fight like that.
- NARRATOR: Now they walk out through a fog, and the steppe is only grass again, 589 years from home.
