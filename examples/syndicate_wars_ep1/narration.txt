Could a gangster from the 1930s survive a world where everybody has a CAMERA? Not a film set. A causeway, a neon sunset, and every phone pointing at you. Welcome to Vice City, in the state of Leonida. Rockstar's next game lives there. And we're about to drop four crime bosses into it. Four bosses. Four ways of ruling. And only one of them understands what a phone camera REALLY is.
Quick honesty first. Rockstar's page gives a release date, November 19, 2026. It gives no year for the story. So the year is OUR assumption. The scenario is our invention. Every word the bosses say here is made up. The history? That part is real. We'll tell you when it is.
Now. Choose your boss. Five stats each, out of five stars. These ratings are OURS. Opinions, not facts. And we'll show our reasons.
First card. Al Capone. Brutality, five stars. Intimidation, four. But adaptability? One star. Tech-savviness? One star. Our reasoning: Capone ran on fear. And fear needs a room to work in. Not a FEED.
Second card. Vito Corleone, the film-era Don. Influence and networking, five stars. Strategy, four. Brutality, just two. Tech-savviness, one. Vito rules with favours, not fists. Judges. Politicians. And a very long memory.
Third card. Haruto Mizuno, our invented Yakuza boss. No real person. No real group. Discipline, five stars. Brutality, four. Adaptability, four. Tech, four. But chaos management? Two. Remember that.
Fourth card. Tommy Shelby, in his 1930s Birmingham years. Adaptability, five. Tech-savviness, five. Strategy, five. Influence, four. Brutality, three. Our reasoning: Shelby treats information like CASH.
So here's what this video asks. In a world where everybody films everything, who realises FIRST that attention is both power and danger? We'll test it on the things Rockstar's games love to joke about. The phone. The feed. The money. The wanted level. In GTA V, those were jokes. For the new game, we can only guess. Four rounds. Loudest first.
Round one. Al Capone. In 1930, Chicago's crime commission names him Public Enemy Number One. And during the Depression, he sponsors a soup kitchen. He's not hiding. He's performing. Our reading? Capone understood that fame is a kind of shield.
Now drop Capone in Vice City. Invented, remember. Someone films him handing out free sandwiches. Millions of views by lunch. Brilliant! Oh, but then someone films the car he arrived in. Then the driver. Then the licence plate. In the 1930s, newspapers needed him as a story. Today, a camera makes everyone one. Attention is power. Right up until it becomes a MAP.
And here's the part history already wrote. What brought Capone down wasn't a rival. Or a bullet. It was a LEDGER. An IRS investigator, Frank J. Wilson, found a line in a book. About seventeen thousand five hundred dollars, paid for Al. In October 1931, Capone was convicted of tax evasion. Eleven years in prison.
Hmm. Capone's mistake would work in any century. He thought controlling people meant controlling INFORMATION. It doesn't. Round one, and he's out. He did end up in Florida, though. At home on Palm Island, in 1947. A quiet ending for a loud man. The next boss never makes a speech.
A quick word on stars. In GTA V, your wanted level climbs when the police see you. In a city of phones, we're guessing it climbs when ANYBODY sees you. The stars in the corner aren't about the police any more. They're about the audience.
Round two. Vito Corleone. A man who wins with favours. Can Vito survive a city where every phone is a witness and nobody owes him ANYTHING?
Vito's business is quiet. His legal front is an olive-oil import company, Genco Pura. His real power is who owes him. Judges. Politicians. And when someone offers Vito the heroin trade, he says no. Because those same judges and politicians would turn on him. That's not morality. That's STRATEGY.
So in Vice City, Vito does the sensible thing. He retreats to his import business. Legal. Boring. Safe. Safe? Well, no. A favour works on people who know you. Strangers with phones don't. One delivery driver films a crate going into a van. The clip spreads. And the judge who owed Vito a favour stops answering his calls. Invented, but it tracks.
Vito doesn't lose a fight. He loses a phone call. Round two. The Don finds out his authority doesn't reach the people his network DEPENDS on. In the story, he dies in his garden in 1955, playing with his grandson. A gentler ending than anyone else here gets. And the next boss doesn't need anyone to love him. Just to sign.
Round three. Haruto Mizuno. Invented, remember. Quiet voice. Slim suit. A business card that says consultant. Can a man with perfect discipline be beaten by a FEED?
Mizuno plays the stock-market way of GTA V. In that game, you can trade stocks through the in-game internet. Fans say there are two exchanges, LCN and BAWSAQ. One is moved by your own actions, the other by other players. Whether the new game has either, nobody outside Rockstar can tell us. We're guessing.
And in our scenario, Mizuno wins the boardroom. Clean contracts. Patient partners. Rivals sign. Rivals smile. Rivals pay. A skyline of buildings that never saw a bad invoice.
Meanwhile, elsewhere in Leonida. A man tries to escape the police on a flamingo-shaped boat. Straight into a car wash. A seagull in a tiny captain's hat takes credit for the arrest. None of this happened. We made it up. But it's Florida, so it feels like it should have. The point? Chaos does not read quarterly reports.
Now picture Mizuno stepping out of the boardroom. A flamingo boat crashes into his lobby. Someone films it. A rumour trends. His biggest partner panics before his lawyers wake up. He wins the boardroom. He loses the STREET. Chaos management, two stars. We did warn you.
Three bosses down. One left. And he's been watching all of this, very closely.
Round four. Tommy Shelby. Can the man who treats information like cash be beaten by a world that shares EVERYTHING?
Right. Shelby never hides from attention. He feeds it. In the story, he becomes an MP. He plants false information to fool a rival. In Vice City, Shelby wouldn't post. He'd pay someone else to post the wrong thing. In the wrong place. A decoy here. The real ledger there. Every phone points at the decoy. And the real business moves.
Is Shelby violent? Yes. The story shows it. An assassination attempt. Worse. But in Vice City, violence is content. So he does the one thing nobody else did. Shelby makes himself BORING on camera.
Shelby's flaw isn't weakness. It's CERTAINTY. He believes he's always several steps ahead. And a man like that never checks behind him. In a city of cameras, behind him is exactly where the cameras are.
So that's the order. Capone first, loud and famous. Vito second. Mizuno third. Shelby last. Quietest of all.
So who saw it first? Who understood that attention cuts both ways? Shelby. That's why he wins. Mostly. But he's the only one who thinks he has it under CONTROL.
