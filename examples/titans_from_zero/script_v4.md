# What If 10 Business Titans Went Broke? Five Years to Prove It Wasn't Luck — final script (v4, 2026-10-20)


## S01  the bench

**NARRATOR:** Monday, nine in the morning. A courthouse hallway in New York, and one long wooden bench.  
**NARRATOR:** Ten men sit on it, shoulder to shoulder, and none of them looks at the others.  
**NARRATOR:** Donald Trump fixes his tie in the black screen of his phone. Robert Kiyosaki is telling the guard that debt is a gift.  
**NARRATOR:** On Friday they owned towers, rockets, studios and stores. By lunch, a clerk will turn all of it into paperwork.  
**NARRATOR:** But nobody will lend them a dollar after today. Not a bank, not a friend, not the man beside them.  
**NARRATOR:** They walk out with one thing: whatever is inside their heads.  
**NARRATOR:** Well, you'd think that's enough to start again. Five years will show if it was ever skill.  
**NARRATOR:** And the best deal of those five years is about to be made on this bench, for forty dollars.  

## S02  forty dollars

**NARRATOR:** At the far end, Kevin O'Leary reads the others one by one, the way a man reads price tags.  
**NARRATOR:** Then he slides along the bench to Elon Musk.  
**O'LEARY:** You can still build things?  
**MUSK:** I can build anything. That isn't the problem.  
**O'LEARY:** So what is?  
**MUSK:** Nobody pays for the parts before the thing exists.  
**O'LEARY:** Then I will. Forty dollars.  
**MUSK:** Forty dollars buys one wheel.  
**O'LEARY:** And ten percent of everything you ever build. Forever.  
**TRUMP:** Ten percent for forty? I've sold air for more.  
**O'LEARY:** Well, then sell me some. I've got an envelope for you too.  
**MUSK:** Why would I sign that?  
**O'LEARY:** Because forty is more than anyone else in this building is offering.  
**NARRATOR:** Musk signs. O'Leary moves one seat along and holds out the next envelope to Jordan Belfort.  
**BELFORT:** Forever is a long word in a contract.  
**O'LEARY:** It's a short one in a bankruptcy.  
**NARRATOR:** Belfort reads it to the last line, folds it, and hands it back. O'Leary stands, a thick stack of white envelopes under his arm.  

## S03  the street

**NARRATOR:** By noon the ten are back on the street, and the city teaches them its first lesson.  
**NARRATOR:** Grant Cardone asks a bank for a small loan. The banker types his name, reads the screen, and slides the form back.  
**NARRATOR:** Robert Kiyosaki asks for a credit card. Same screen. Same slide.  
**NARRATOR:** Donald Trump taps his card at a coffee cart, and the machine beeps twice.  
**NARRATOR:** Right. A loan was only ever a stranger's trust with interest on top. And nobody trusts them for a cent.  
**NARRATOR:** So the only money they can touch is money they make today, with their hands or with their mouths.  
**NARRATOR:** Musk has forty dollars and a parts list. Jordan Belfort has a phone with no numbers in it.  

## S04  the parking lot

**NARRATOR:** A week later, a supermarket parking lot. Musk has built one good scooter out of three broken ones.  
**NARRATOR:** For six hours he has explained its battery to everyone who walks by. Nobody stops.  
**NARRATOR:** Belfort watches from the curb with a hot dog. Then he takes the handlebars without asking.  
**NARRATOR:** He never mentions the battery. He lets a delivery rider take one free lap of the lot, then names three times Musk's price.  
**NARRATOR:** The rider pays cash.  
**BELFORT:** You were selling a battery. I sold him an hour of his evening.  
**MUSK:** It's the same scooter.  
**BELFORT:** Not to him. He rode it before he paid.  
**MUSK:** So you gave it away first.  
**BELFORT:** Give a little first. People hate owing you.  
**MUSK:** What do you want?  
**BELFORT:** Half. You build, I sell.  
**MUSK:** Ten percent already belongs to a man with envelopes.  
**BELFORT:** Okay, then forty-five each. I won't even ask.  
**MUSK:** One rule. You never promise what I haven't built.  
**BELFORT:** Me? Never.  
**NARRATOR:** They shake hands. Neither of them lets go first.  

## S05  the motel

**NARRATOR:** Across the river, a motel by the highway rents rooms by the week. Two men split the cheapest one.  
**KIYOSAKI:** Debt is how the rich get richer. Write that down.  
**TRUMP:** Fantastic. Lend me some.  
**KIYOSAKI:** Well, I don't have any.  
**TRUMP:** Then you don't own the thing you're selling.  
**KIYOSAKI:** Banks forget. In a year they'll beg to lend us.  
**TRUMP:** My companies went to bankruptcy court six times. The banks never came back first.  
**KIYOSAKI:** So who did?  
**TRUMP:** The cameras.  
**KIYOSAKI:** Cameras don't pay rent.  
**TRUMP:** Watch.  
**NARRATOR:** Trump props his phone on a plastic chair by the empty pool and goes live.  
**NARRATOR:** Eleven strangers watch him eat a vending machine sandwich with a knife and fork, like a state dinner.  
**NARRATOR:** By midnight, eleven is four thousand. Kiyosaki calls the bank again and gets the voicemail.  

## S06  six months

**NARRATOR:** Six months later, the garage behind the motel smells of solder.  
**NARRATOR:** Musk sleeps on a folding cot beside the workbench and rebuilds thirty scooters a month. Every one leaves with Belfort.  
**NARRATOR:** Belfort never says lithium. If you deliver food for a living, he sells you one more delivery an hour.  
**NARRATOR:** Across town, Alex Hormozi walks into dying gyms and offers to fill them. He gets paid only from new members, cash first.  
**NARRATOR:** Mark Zuckerberg sits in a public library writing code that finds broken shop websites. Then he fixes them for a fee.  
**NARRATOR:** Mark Cuban rents one bicycle to a kid who delivers lunch. Then two bicycles. Then twenty.  
**NARRATOR:** Jeff Bezos sells used books off a folding table and writes down every title that sells twice.  
**NARRATOR:** But Grant Cardone, making a hundred calls a day, gets a hundred hang-ups.  
**NARRATOR:** His deals run on other people's money. His name can't raise it anymore.  

## S07  the live show

**NARRATOR:** By month nine, a hundred thousand people tune in every night to watch Trump eat cereal from a crystal glass.  
**NARRATOR:** Uh-oh. One night, Kiyosaki steps into the frame.  
**KIYOSAKI:** Mind if I teach them something useful?  
**TRUMP:** Two minutes. They come for me, not homework.  
**KIYOSAKI:** Lesson one. Your house is not an asset.  
**TRUMP:** You're losing them. Nine hundred gone. Two thousand.  
**KIYOSAKI:** They need to hear this.  
**TRUMP:** They need to hear me.  
**NARRATOR:** Trump turns the phone. Kiyosaki is out of the picture, and the count climbs back in seconds.  

## S08  five hundred

**NARRATOR:** Year one. Mark Cuban runs two hundred delivery riders out of a warehouse office with no chairs.  
**NARRATOR:** He has heard about the scooters that never break down.  
**CUBAN:** How many can you make by Christmas?  
**MUSK:** Fifty. Fifty good ones.  
**BELFORT:** Five hundred.  
**MUSK:** We have parts for fifty.  
**BELFORT:** He's modest. It's a condition.  
**CUBAN:** Five hundred by the twentieth of December, or you never walk in here again.  
**CUBAN:** Can you?  
**MUSK:** Yes.  
**NARRATOR:** Cuban signs. It is the biggest order either of them has seen in a year.  

## S09  the van

**NARRATOR:** In the parking lot, Musk doesn't start the van.  
**MUSK:** You promised what I haven't built.  
**BELFORT:** Look, you said yes. I heard you.  
**MUSK:** Because you'd already said it.  
**BELFORT:** That's how big orders work. Promise, then build.  
**MUSK:** That's how fires work.  
**NARRATOR:** Belfort laughs. Then he sees Musk isn't laughing, and stops.  
**NARRATOR:** For eleven weeks the garage lights never go off.  
**NARRATOR:** The good cells won't arrive before Christmas, so Musk orders cheap ones from a seller he has never met.  
**NARRATOR:** A heat test takes a day per batch. He skips it on the last two hundred.  
**NARRATOR:** On the nineteenth of December, five hundred scooters roll into Cuban's warehouse. One day early.  
**NARRATOR:** Cuban pays. Belfort frames the receipt. Three hundred cheap cells are left over on a garage shelf.  

## S10  the limousine

**NARRATOR:** Year two. A black car waits outside the motel office.  
**TRUMP:** A network wants a show. How to Lose a Billion. Season one.  
**KIYOSAKI:** And what will you own, exactly?  
**TRUMP:** The audience. The rest is paperwork.  
**KIYOSAKI:** An audience isn't an asset.  
**TRUMP:** It paid for this car.  
**KIYOSAKI:** A car is a liability.  
**TRUMP:** Not when somebody else pays for it.  
**TRUMP:** There's a seat in the back. Season one needs a man who lost everything.  
**KIYOSAKI:** I'm not a lesson. I teach them.  
**TRUMP:** Well, suit yourself.  
**NARRATOR:** The car pulls away. Kiyosaki carries his bag to an old Toyota at the far end of the lot.  
**NARRATOR:** He slept in one once, a long time ago, with Kim. He still knows which seat folds flat.  

## S11  the fire

**NARRATOR:** Year three. Two in the morning. The garage behind the motel is dark.  
**NARRATOR:** On the shelf, one of the three hundred leftover cells starts to hiss.  
**NARRATOR:** By the time the fire trucks arrive, the garage is a black box without a roof. Nobody is hurt. Everything else is gone.  
**MUSK:** Those cells came from the same box as Cuban's.  
**BELFORT:** Wait, Cuban's have run for two years. They're fine.  
**MUSK:** Two hundred of them never had the heat test.  
**BELFORT:** You never told me that.  
**MUSK:** I told you we had parts for fifty.  
**BELFORT:** You said it quietly.  
**MUSK:** You never listen when it's quiet.  
**BELFORT:** If Cuban hears this, we're finished.  
**MUSK:** If a rider's scooter burns, we're worse than finished.  
**NARRATOR:** Belfort looks at the black shelf for a long time. He doesn't answer.  
**NARRATOR:** Replacing two hundred batteries would cost more than they have left.  

## S12  the man in grey

**NARRATOR:** Two days later, a man in a grey suit steps over the ashes, careful of his shoes, and holds out a card. Vance.  
**VANCE:** I'll buy the company today. You're both rich by Friday.  
**BELFORT:** What's the catch?  
**VANCE:** The fire was old wiring in an old garage. Nothing to do with batteries.  
**MUSK:** Two hundred riders are sitting on those batteries.  
**VANCE:** And they'll be fine. Probably.  
**VANCE:** You know how this kind of paper works.  
**BELFORT:** Yeah, I signed paper like that once. I paid for it in a cell.  
**VANCE:** Then you know it works.  
**BELFORT:** That's why I'm not signing.  
**NARRATOR:** Vance leaves his card on the burnt workbench.  
**NARRATOR:** Belfort picks up his phone and does something he has never done in a sale. He calls the buyer to tell him what's wrong.  

## S13  everything in

**NARRATOR:** The next morning, Cuban's warehouse. Two hundred scooters stand in rows.  
**BELFORT:** Two hundred of your batteries skipped a test. We want them back.  
**CUBAN:** Are they bad?  
**MUSK:** Probably not. I don't sell probably.  
**CUBAN:** Who pays for two hundred new ones?  
**MUSK:** We do.  
**BELFORT:** With what?  
**MUSK:** With everything. I did it once before, on a Christmas Eve.  
**CUBAN:** Nobody has ever come in here to give me money back.  
**NARRATOR:** Belfort opens his mouth to make a pitch. For once, he closes it.  
**CUBAN:** Fix them. Then bring me next year's number. A real one.  
**NARRATOR:** They spend every dollar on two hundred new batteries, and Musk tests each one twice, on camera. Then they post the fire report online, every page.  
**NARRATOR:** For three months they eat from the motel vending machine. Belfort calls it the executive menu.  

## S14  year five

**NARRATOR:** Year five. Cuban's riders run on five thousand of their batteries, and not one has burned.  
**NARRATOR:** Belfort's whole pitch is one sentence now. Ours burned once. That's why they don't.  
**NARRATOR:** Grant Cardone works their phones. Same calls as year one. Now people pick up, because the name on the box means something.  
**NARRATOR:** Hormozi runs sixty gyms on cash. Zuckerberg sold his little website robot and vanished into a bigger one.  
**NARRATOR:** Trump is filming season four of How to Lose a Billion. Last season he read their fire report on air, page by page.  
**NARRATOR:** He called it the only honest paper in America. Ratings doubled.  
**NARRATOR:** And Kiyosaki is in the motel lobby, explaining to the night clerk why debt is a gift. The clerk has heard it before.  
**NARRATOR:** One viewer read every page after him. Then he walked into their office.  
**BEZOS:** I read your fire report. All of it.  
**BELFORT:** Most buyers ask us to bury it.  
**BEZOS:** That's why I'm offering double what they would.  
**MUSK:** Double.  
**BELFORT:** Make it a partnership, and you can buy lunch.  

## S15  the envelopes

**NARRATOR:** Five years after the bench, Musk and Belfort walk into the same courthouse to register the deal.  
**NARRATOR:** Oh, the bench is still there. So is Kevin O'Leary.  
**O'LEARY:** Ten percent. Remember?  
**MUSK:** Of a burnt garage?  
**O'LEARY:** Of everything. Fires included. Read the envelope.  
**BELFORT:** Well, you got lucky with him.  
**O'LEARY:** I didn't pick him.  
**NARRATOR:** Belfort's grin stays on a second too long.  
**NARRATOR:** He opens his briefcase. Eight signed envelopes. Trump. Kiyosaki. Cardone. Hormozi. Cuban. Bezos. Zuckerberg. Musk.  
**BELFORT:** Eight?  
**O'LEARY:** Nine offered. One man read to the last line and said no.  
**BELFORT:** I read contracts.  
**O'LEARY:** Then you went into business with a man who doesn't.  
**NARRATOR:** Musk looks at the floor. Belfort looks at Musk.  
**O'LEARY:** Most of these paid back my forty. Three of them paid for everything.  
**MUSK:** So it was luck.  
**O'LEARY:** Luck chose the three. I made sure I owned all eight.  

## S16  the verdict

**NARRATOR:** Ten men walked out of this building with nothing but what they knew.  
**NARRATOR:** The ones who could build or sell with their own hands were back within months. The ones who lived on other people's trust are still waiting.  
**NARRATOR:** And the one who never built or sold anything spread forty dollars across eight of them.  
**NARRATOR:** Luck decided which envelope paid. Skill decided who was still standing when it did.  
**NARRATOR:** So if you sat on that bench tomorrow, which envelope would be yours?
