# Titans from Zero: full script, words (v2, 2026-10-20)

**Title:** What If 10 Business Titans Went Broke? Five Years to Prove It Wasn't Luck

**Spoken words:** 2034 (about 12.3 minutes at the narrator's pace, plus the pauses of the scenes)
NARRATOR lines are in plain text; dialogue is NAME (to whom): line.

## S01 the bench (day0)

- Monday, nine in the morning. A courthouse hallway in New York, and one long wooden bench.
- Ten men sit on it, shoulder to shoulder, and none of them looks at the others.
- Donald Trump fixes his tie in the black screen of his phone. Robert Kiyosaki is telling the guard that debt is a gift.
- On Friday they owned towers, rockets, studios and stores. By lunch, a clerk will turn all of it into paperwork.
- But nobody will lend them a dollar after today. Not a bank, not a friend, not the man beside them.
- They walk out with one thing: whatever is inside their heads.
- You'd think that's enough to start again. Five years will show if it was ever skill.
- And the best deal of those five years is about to be made on this bench, for forty dollars.

## S02 forty dollars (day0)
*Scene: the bankruptcy court hallway, the long bench. O'LEARY, MUSK, TRUMP, BELFORT.*

- At the far end, Kevin O'Leary reads the others one by one, the way a man reads price tags.
- Then he slides along the bench to Elon Musk.
- **O'LEARY** (MUSK): You can still build things?
- **MUSK** (O'LEARY): I can build anything. That isn't the problem.
- **O'LEARY** (MUSK): So what is?
- **MUSK** (O'LEARY): Nobody pays for the parts before the thing exists.
- **O'LEARY** (MUSK): Then I will. Forty dollars.
- **MUSK** (O'LEARY): Forty dollars buys one wheel.
- **O'LEARY** (MUSK): And ten percent of everything you ever build. Forever.
- **TRUMP** (room): Ten percent for forty? I've sold air for more.
- **O'LEARY** (TRUMP): Then sell me some. I've got an envelope for you too.
- **MUSK** (O'LEARY): Why would I sign that?
- **O'LEARY** (MUSK): Because forty is more than anyone else in this building is offering.
- Musk signs. O'Leary moves one seat along and holds out the next envelope to Jordan Belfort.
- **BELFORT** (O'LEARY): Forever is a long word in a contract.
- **O'LEARY** (BELFORT): It's a short one in a bankruptcy.
- Belfort reads it to the last line, folds it, and hands it back. O'Leary stands, a thick stack of white envelopes under his arm.

## S03 the street (day1)

- By noon the ten are back on the street, and the city teaches them its first lesson.
- Grant Cardone asks a bank for a small loan. The banker types his name, reads the screen, and slides the form back.
- Robert Kiyosaki asks for a credit card. Same screen. Same slide.
- Donald Trump taps his card at a coffee cart, and the machine beeps twice.
- Here's what you learn fast on that street. A loan was only ever a stranger's trust with interest on top. And nobody trusts them for a cent.
- So the only money they can touch is money they make today, with their hands or with their mouths.
- Musk has forty dollars and a parts list. Jordan Belfort has a phone with no numbers in it.

## S04 the parking lot (week1)
*Scene: a supermarket parking lot, late afternoon. MUSK, BELFORT.*

- A week later, a supermarket parking lot. Musk has built one good scooter out of three broken ones.
- For six hours he has explained its battery to everyone who walks by. Nobody stops.
- Belfort watches from the curb with a hot dog. Then he takes the handlebars without asking.
- He never mentions the battery. He lets a delivery rider take one free lap of the lot, then names three times Musk's price.
- The rider pays cash.
- **BELFORT** (MUSK): You were selling a battery. I sold him an hour of his evening.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): It's the same scooter.
- **BELFORT** (MUSK): Not to him. He rode it before he paid.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): So you gave it away first.
- **BELFORT** (MUSK): Give a little first. People hate owing you.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): What do you want?
- **BELFORT** (MUSK): Half. You build, I sell.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): Ten percent already belongs to a man with envelopes.
- **BELFORT** (MUSK): Then forty-five each. I won't even ask.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): One rule. You never promise what I haven't built.
- **BELFORT** (MUSK): Me? Never.
- They shake hands. Neither of them lets go first.

## S05 the motel (day1)
*Scene: a cheap highway motel room, then the empty pool. KIYOSAKI, TRUMP.*

- Across the river, a motel by the highway rents rooms by the week. Two men split the cheapest one.
- **KIYOSAKI** (TRUMP): Debt is how the rich get richer. Write that down.
- **TRUMP** (KIYOSAKI): Fantastic. Lend me some.
- **KIYOSAKI** (TRUMP): I don't have any.
- **TRUMP** (KIYOSAKI): Then you don't own the thing you're selling.
- **KIYOSAKI** (TRUMP): Banks forget. In a year they'll beg to lend us.
- **TRUMP** (KIYOSAKI): My companies went to bankruptcy court six times. The banks never came back first.
- **KIYOSAKI** (TRUMP): So who did?
- **TRUMP** (KIYOSAKI): The cameras.
- **KIYOSAKI** (TRUMP): Cameras don't pay rent.
- **TRUMP** (KIYOSAKI): Watch.
- Trump props his phone on a plastic chair by the empty pool and goes live.
- Eleven strangers watch him eat a vending machine sandwich with a knife and fork, like a state dinner.
- By midnight, eleven is four thousand. Kiyosaki calls the bank again and gets the voicemail.

## S06 six months (month6)

- Six months later, the garage behind the motel smells of solder.
- Musk sleeps on a folding cot beside the workbench and rebuilds thirty scooters a month. Every one leaves with Belfort.
- Belfort never says lithium. If you deliver food for a living, he sells you one more delivery an hour.
- Across town, Alex Hormozi walks into dying gyms and offers to fill them. He gets paid only from new members, cash first.
- Mark Zuckerberg sits in a public library writing code that finds broken shop websites. Then he fixes them for a fee.
- Mark Cuban rents one bicycle to a kid who delivers lunch. Then two bicycles. Then twenty.
- Jeff Bezos sells used books off a folding table and writes down every title that sells twice.
- But Grant Cardone, making a hundred calls a day, gets a hundred hang-ups.
- His plan pools other people's money into a building. And the one thing his name can't raise anymore is their trust.

## S07 the live show (month9)
*Scene: the motel pool at night, on a live video. KIYOSAKI, TRUMP.*

- By month nine, a hundred thousand people tune in every night to watch Trump eat cereal from a crystal glass.
- One night, Kiyosaki steps into the frame.
- **KIYOSAKI** (TRUMP): Mind if I teach them something useful?
- **TRUMP** (KIYOSAKI): Two minutes. They come for me, not homework.
- **KIYOSAKI** (room): Lesson one. Your house is not an asset.
- **TRUMP** (KIYOSAKI): You're losing them. Nine hundred gone. Two thousand.
- **KIYOSAKI** (TRUMP): They need to hear this.
- **TRUMP** (KIYOSAKI): They need to hear me.
- Trump turns the phone. Kiyosaki is out of the picture, and the count climbs back in seconds.

## S08 five hundred (year1)
*Scene: Cuban's warehouse office, no chairs. CUBAN, MUSK, BELFORT.*

- Year one. Mark Cuban runs two hundred delivery riders out of a warehouse office with no chairs.
- He has heard about the scooters that never break down.
- **CUBAN** (MUSK): How many can you make by Christmas?
- **MUSK** (CUBAN): Fifty. Fifty good ones.
- **BELFORT** (CUBAN): Five hundred.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): We have parts for fifty.
- **BELFORT** (CUBAN): He's modest. It's a condition.
- **CUBAN** (BELFORT): Five hundred by the twentieth of December, or you never walk in here again.
- **CUBAN** (MUSK): Can you?
- **MUSK** (CUBAN): Yes.
- Cuban signs. It is the biggest order either of them has seen in a year.

## S09 the van (year1)
*Scene: the van in Cuban's parking lot. MUSK, BELFORT.*

- In the parking lot, Musk doesn't start the van.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): You promised what I haven't built.
- **BELFORT** (MUSK): You said yes. I heard you.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): Because you'd already said it.
- **BELFORT** (MUSK): That's how big orders work. Promise, then build.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): That's how fires work.
- Belfort laughs. Then he sees Musk isn't laughing, and stops.
- For eleven weeks the garage lights never go off.
- The good cells won't arrive before Christmas, so Musk orders cheap ones from a seller he has never met.
- A heat test takes a day per batch. He skips it on the last two hundred.
- On the nineteenth of December, five hundred scooters roll into Cuban's warehouse. One day early.
- Cuban pays. Belfort frames the receipt. Three hundred cheap cells are left over on a garage shelf.

## S10 the limousine (year2)
*Scene: the motel parking lot, beside a black car. TRUMP, KIYOSAKI.*

- Year two. A black car waits outside the motel office.
- **TRUMP** (KIYOSAKI): A network wants a show. How to Lose a Billion. Season one.
- **KIYOSAKI** (TRUMP): And what will you own, exactly?
- **TRUMP** (KIYOSAKI): The audience. The rest is paperwork.
- **KIYOSAKI** (TRUMP): An audience isn't an asset.
- **TRUMP** (KIYOSAKI): It paid for this car.
- **KIYOSAKI** (TRUMP): A car is a liability.
- **TRUMP** (KIYOSAKI): Not when somebody else pays for it.
- The car pulls away. Kiyosaki carries his bag to an old Toyota at the far end of the lot.
- He slept in one once, a long time ago, with Kim. He still knows which seat folds flat.

## S11 the fire (year3)
*Scene: the burnt-out garage at dawn, ash on everything. MUSK, BELFORT.*

- Year three. Two in the morning. The garage behind the motel is dark.
- On the shelf, one of the three hundred leftover cells starts to hiss.
- By the time the fire trucks arrive, the garage is a black box without a roof. Nobody is hurt. Everything else is gone.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): Those cells came from the same box as Cuban's.
- **BELFORT** (MUSK): Cuban's have run for two years. They're fine.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): Two hundred of them never had the heat test.
- **BELFORT** (MUSK): You never told me that.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): I told you we had parts for fifty.
- **BELFORT** (MUSK): You said it quietly.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): You never listen when it's quiet.
- **BELFORT** (MUSK): If Cuban hears this, we're finished.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): If a rider's scooter burns, we're worse than finished.
- Belfort looks at the black shelf for a long time. He doesn't answer.
- Replacing two hundred batteries would cost more than they have left.

## S12 the man in grey (year3)
*Scene: the burnt garage, two days later. VANCE, BELFORT, MUSK.*

- Two days later, a man in a grey suit steps over the ashes. His name is Vance, and he buys companies before their bad week.
- **VANCE** (BELFORT): I'll buy the company today. You're both rich by Friday.
- **BELFORT** (VANCE): What's the catch?
- **VANCE** (BELFORT): The fire was old wiring in an old garage. Nothing to do with batteries.
- **MUSK** (VANCE): Two hundred riders are sitting on those batteries.
- **VANCE** (MUSK): And they'll be fine. Probably.
- **VANCE** (BELFORT): You know how this kind of paper works.
- **BELFORT** (VANCE): I signed paper like that once. I paid for it in a cell.
- **VANCE** (BELFORT): Then you know it works.
- **BELFORT** (VANCE): That's why I'm not signing.
- Vance leaves his card on the burnt workbench.
- Belfort picks up his phone and does something he has never done in a sale. He calls the buyer to tell him what's wrong.

## S13 everything in (year3)
*Scene: Cuban's warehouse, between rows of scooters. BELFORT, CUBAN, MUSK.*

- The next morning, Cuban's warehouse. Two hundred scooters stand in rows.
- **BELFORT** (CUBAN): Two hundred of your batteries skipped a test. We want them back.
- **CUBAN** (MUSK): Are they bad?
- **MUSK** (CUBAN): Probably not. I don't sell probably.
- **CUBAN** (BELFORT): Who pays for two hundred new ones?
- **MUSK** (CUBAN): We do.
- **BELFORT** (MUSK): With what?
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): With everything. I did it once before, on a Christmas Eve.
- **CUBAN** (BELFORT): Nobody has ever come in here to give me money back.
- Belfort opens his mouth to make a pitch. For once, he closes it.
- **CUBAN** (MUSK): Fix them. Then bring me next year's number. A real one.
- They spend every dollar on two hundred new batteries, and Musk tests each one twice, on camera.
- For three months they eat from the motel vending machine. Belfort calls it the executive menu.

## S14 year five (year5)
*Scene: the new factory office, a framed receipt on the wall. BEZOS, BELFORT, MUSK.*

- Year five. Cuban's riders run on five thousand of their batteries, and not one has burned.
- Belfort's whole pitch is one sentence now. Ours burned once. That's why they don't.
- Grant Cardone works their phones. Same calls as year one. Now people pick up, because the name on the box means something.
- Hormozi runs sixty gyms on cash. Zuckerberg sold his little website robot and vanished into a bigger one.
- Trump is filming season four of How to Lose a Billion, and every season he loses it more profitably.
- And Kiyosaki is in the motel lobby, explaining to the night clerk why debt is a gift. The clerk has heard it before.
- Then a buyer walks in who has read every page of the fire report.
- **BEZOS** (BELFORT): I read your fire report. All of it.
- **BELFORT** (BEZOS): Most buyers ask us to bury it.
- **BEZOS** (BELFORT): That's why I'm offering double what they would.
- **MUSK** (BELFORT): Double.
- **BELFORT** (BEZOS): Make it a partnership, and you can buy lunch.

## S15 the envelopes (year5)
*Scene: the courthouse hallway, the same bench. O'LEARY, MUSK, BELFORT.*

- Five years after the bench, Musk and Belfort walk into the same courthouse to register the deal.
- The bench is still there. So is Kevin O'Leary.
- **O'LEARY** (MUSK): Ten percent. Remember?
- **MUSK** (O'LEARY): Of a burnt garage?
- **O'LEARY** (MUSK): Of everything. Fires included. Read the envelope.
- **BELFORT** (O'LEARY): You got lucky with him.
- **O'LEARY** (BELFORT): I didn't pick him.
- Belfort's grin stays on a second too long.
- He opens his briefcase. Eight signed envelopes. Trump. Kiyosaki. Cardone. Hormozi. Cuban. Bezos. Zuckerberg. Musk.
- **BELFORT** (O'LEARY): Eight?
- **O'LEARY** (BELFORT): Nine offered. One man read to the last line and said no.
- **BELFORT** (O'LEARY): I read contracts.
- **O'LEARY** (BELFORT): Then you went into business with a man who doesn't.
- Musk looks at the floor. Belfort looks at Musk.
- **O'LEARY** (MUSK): Most of these paid back my forty. Three of them paid for everything.
- **MUSK** (O'LEARY): So it was luck.
- **O'LEARY** (MUSK): Luck chose the three. I made sure I owned all eight.

## S16 the verdict (year5)

- Ten men walked out of this building with nothing but what they knew.
- The ones whose skill ran on other people's trust waited years for it. Some are still waiting.
- The ones who could build or sell with their own hands were back within months.
- And the one who never built or sold anything spread forty dollars across eight of them.
- Luck decided which envelope paid. Skill decided who was still standing when it did.
- So if you sat on that bench tomorrow, which envelope would be yours?
